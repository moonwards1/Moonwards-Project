The system

a moon's orbit areostationary prime meridian

Mars-centred view, to scale, with Mars's north pole up. Mars turns once in 24 h 37 min 23 s about its real pole, and the grid on it is every 30° of latitude and longitude. Phobos and Deimos follow precessing ellipses built from JPL's mean elements: Phobos's orbit is tilted 1.08° to Mars's equator and that tilt swings round once in 2.26 years; its distance from Mars's centre varies between 9,234 and 9,518 km over each orbit. Deimos's orbit swings round a plane 0.9° off the equator once in 56 years, so its tilt to the equator is anywhere from 0.9° to 2.7°. These give the shape and motion of the system, not a forecast of where along its orbit a moon will be on a given date in the 2080s. The Sun is at its true direction and distance. The three views hold different things still: the stars, the ground of Mars, or Phobos. The facilities and the flights between them are added in later steps.
