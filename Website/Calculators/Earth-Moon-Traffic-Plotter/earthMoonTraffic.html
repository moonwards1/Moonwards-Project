Every facility follows the clock: the release time decides which way the tether points when it lets go. A ship can be caught anywhere along the destination tether, by a catcher that moves along it and absorbs up to 800 m/s of relative motion. With a destination chosen, Target (under the view) adjusts the release time and altitude until the ship meets that tether at the point where the relative motion is least.

Geocentric Earth–Moon view. The Moon is placed from a Meeus low-precision lunar ephemeris; the Sun is at its true position and distance. Each skyhook is a hanging (gravity-gradient) tether in its body's equatorial plane, turning once per orbit, so a point at radius r moves at ωr. Its angle at any instant follows a clock started on 2080-01-01. The released path is integrated (RK4) under Earth + Moon + Sun gravity; a release that stays inside the Moon's sphere of influence is drawn as its two-body ellipse, carried with the Moon. The Flight slider moves the whole scene to that time after release, and is linear in time; with a destination it runs to half an hour past the flight's closest pass to that body; dragging the ship in the view moves it by distance along its path instead. A flight with a destination is judged at its closest pass to that tether: the miss is the distance between ship and tether there, and the relative motion is the ship's speed relative to that point of the tether. A catcher that moves along the tether takes hold of the ship there if the relative motion is no more than 800 m/s. Target changes only the release time and altitude, and brings the ship to the point of the tether where the relative motion is least; how low that is depends on the date. The marks under the two date sliders are the release windows for the flight's direction: the Earth skyhook turns in Earth's equatorial plane, which the Moon passes through twice in each lunar orbit, so a ship for the Moon has to arrive around one of those crossings and a ship for the Earth skyhook has to leave around one. Green marks are windows where the relative motion at the catch is expected to be within the catcher's limit, amber ones where it is not. A waypoint burn is an instant change of velocity at its time since release, up to 300 m/s along each of the prograde, radial and normal axes, measured against the Moon inside its sphere of influence and against Earth elsewhere; Target leaves the burns as they are and solves around them. Solve far burn is for a flight to the Earth skyhook that first loops out past the Moon's orbit: it keeps the release and sets both waypoints, a burn far out, near where the path crosses Earth's equatorial plane, and a small trim a day before the arrival, so that the ship reaches the tether at perigee moving as the tether does.
