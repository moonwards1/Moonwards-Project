Geocentric Earth–Moon view. The Moon is placed from a Meeus low-precision lunar ephemeris; the Sun is at its true position and distance. Each skyhook is a hanging (gravity-gradient) tether in its body's equatorial plane, turning once per orbit, so a point at radius r moves at ωr. Its angle at any instant follows a clock started on 2080-01-01. The released path is integrated (RK4) under Earth + Moon + Sun gravity; a release that stays inside the Moon's sphere of influence is drawn as its two-body ellipse, carried with the Moon. The Flight slider moves the whole scene to that time after release. A flight with a destination is judged at its closest pass to that tether: the miss is the distance between ship and tether there, and the trim burn is the ship's speed relative to that point of the tether. Target changes only the release time and altitude; it does not reduce the trim burn, which depends on the date and the catch altitude.
